- System Failures == Normal business is impossible

11.1 Dependability Properties 
(they are the later section titles of this chapter)

Dependability is not a factor that is useful to express numerically. Has more to do with the user's feelign that the program will work without issue.

Other factors:
- Repairability: ability to overcome system failures
- Maintainability: ability to accomodate new requirements
- Survivability: ability to continue service while partially disabled or under attack
- Error tolerance: extent that system can handle or avoid errors from user input or other malformed data.


The relationship between dependability and cost is nonlinear - cost increases exponentially.


11.2 Availability & Reliability
- Available: probability it will be running and usable at any given point in time
- Reliable: probability over time that it will correctly deliver expected services

Good to express as numbers 0-1:
- Availability: 0.9 == 90% available over given time period. 0.999 == 99.9% available over given time period.
- Reliability: represents failure rate, so 0.01 would be 1 in every 100 occurances cause a failure. 0.0001 == 1 in 10,000 causes failure

Availability % can be more or less relevant depending on when the system is down. Consider that a short period of downtime when many people want to use it is typically worse than a longer period when very few people want to use it.


11.3 Safety
- Safe: how likely the system is to damage people/it's environment

Degrees of safety-critical software:
- Primary: software embedded as controller, malfunctions will cause human injury or environmental damage.
- Secondary: can indirectly result in an injury. bad calculations resulting in faulty designs that later cause injury also fall in this category.




11.4 Security
- How likely the system can resist intrusions (deliberate or otherwise)

Main security threats to system/data:
1. confidentiality
2. integrity
3. availability
